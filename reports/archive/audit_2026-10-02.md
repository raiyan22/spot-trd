Stop realism: on the 48 days a stop-type exit happened, the day's low traded below the stop by a median 1.08% (mean 2.41%, 90th percentile 6.85%, worst 16.67%). 73% of those days pierced the stop-limit's 0.5% limit price, where a stop-LIMIT sell may not fill at all (the backtest assumes it does; 35 blow-throughs counted). The low is a bound, not the fill price, so 0.1-0.5% stop slippage brackets the evidence.

**Hourly check (48 stop-type exits).** Within the first hour that traded through the stop, the hour's low was below the stop by a median 0.49% (mean 0.90%, 90th percentile 2.00%, worst 7.68%). In 23 of 48 cases price dropped more than the stop-limit's 0.5% band inside that single hour, and in 2 the hour even closed below the limit price: those are the cases where a stop-limit could rest unfilled. Candles can't show the exact fill; treat 0.1% as the typical case and the 0.5% and 1.5% rows as the tail.

## 6. Monte Carlo

Resampling the observed per-trade returns (fraction of equity at entry) with a Poisson number of trades per year. 41 trades is a small sample: these ranges describe the shape of the risk, not a forecast.
